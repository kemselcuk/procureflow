### Product rules

- Four company-scoped roles: employee, department manager, finance approver and company administrator. An individual can hold multiple explicit role assignments, but the requester cannot approve their own request and the two approval steps must have different actors.
- An employee creates and edits their own draft, then submits a positive EUR amount with at most two decimal places, a department, title and justification. Use decimal arithmetic; there is no real purchase/payment integration.
- A request belongs to the requester's active assigned department when submitted; a client cannot select an unrelated department. Submission revalidates membership/department, and later membership changes never rewrite a submitted request. Amounts and budget values must fit the agreed database decimal range; reject excess precision or overflow rather than rounding silently.
- Amounts up to and including EUR 1,000 require department-manager approval. Larger amounts require department-manager approval followed by finance approval. The threshold is fixed initially.
- Submitted request fields are immutable. A requester can cancel a pending request; an authorized current-step approver can reject with a reason. Rejected/cancelled requests are terminal; corrections use a new draft. Approved requests cannot be cancelled in this release.
- States: draft, awaiting department approval, awaiting finance approval, approved, rejected, cancelled. Each transition has one allowed actor/action set; invalid or stale actions return an explicit conflict.
- Fictional department budgets use EUR and calendar months in UTC. Budget usage is charged to the month of **final approval**, using one server-side instant per command. Requests crossing a month boundary use the new month's budget; no reservation or automatic rollover exists.
- Final approval checks the current month's budget and changes usage, request state, approval history, audit history and outbox records in one database transaction. Missing/insufficient budgets prevent the approval and leave those business effects unchanged.
- Company administrators create departments, manage memberships for existing authenticated identities and set monthly limits. They cannot reduce a limit below already used funds or rewrite usage. They gain no implicit approval override. Keycloak account/password administration stays in Keycloak's local administration UI.
- Employees see their own requests/history; managers see their department's approval queue and permitted budget information; finance sees company finance work; company administrators see company configuration and audit information. A role matrix will turn these boundaries into explicit endpoint tests in P1.
- Two seeded companies and at least two departments per company provide distinct employee, manager, finance and administrator identities, threshold-boundary examples and an insufficient-budget example. Fixtures are repeatable and enabled only for local/demo use.
- Notifications cover submission/current approval assignments and terminal outcomes for the relevant recipients. Recipients, event types and in-app/email expectations are frozen as a contract in P2 before worker implementation.

### State and access contract

The following is the proposed contract to implement after approval. A held role grants only its explicit company/department scope; holding several roles never removes separation-of-duty checks.

| State | Allowed command and actor | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft | Requester edits or submits their own request | Edit stays draft; valid submission awaits department approval |
| Awaiting department approval | Assigned department manager, excluding requester, approves or rejects; requester may cancel | Approval at or below EUR 1,000 is final; higher amounts await finance; reject/cancel is terminal |
| Awaiting finance approval | Company finance approver, excluding requester and the prior approver, approves or rejects; requester may cancel | Approval is final; reject/cancel is terminal |
| Approved, rejected or cancelled | Authorized readers view details/history | No further business transition; approved requests cannot be refunded or cancelled |

Final approval succeeds only when the budget check succeeds atomically; missing/insufficient budget leaves the current pending state unchanged. Stale/conflicting commands return a stable conflict code. Repeated successful approval must return the existing result or a documented conflict without duplicate effects. Other API retry semantics, including draft creation and submission, are frozen in P2.

| Role | Read scope | Business authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employee | Own requests/timeline and own notifications | Create/edit/submit own drafts and cancel own pending requests |
| Department manager | Requests/timelines and budgets in assigned departments | Approve/reject the department step within that scope |
| Finance approver | Company finance queue, submitted-request timelines needed for finance work, and company budgets | Approve/reject the finance step |
| Company administrator | Company configuration and audit; no blanket access to other users' draft contents | Manage departments, memberships/role assignments and monthly budget limits; no implicit approval authority |

Every active member may read only their own notification records. Inactive membership denies application access even with an unexpired JWT. P1 turns this contract into an endpoint matrix; each later phase extends its negative tests when those endpoints exist.

## Proposed architecture and ownership

These are future paths; this plan does not create the application tree.

- `backend/app/`: the main Spring Boot application. Package modules own identity/membership, requests, approvals, budgets, audit, notifications and outbox relay. Cross-module calls use explicit application services/contracts, not another module's repositories.
- `backend/event-contracts/`: the small versioned event envelope and payload types shared by the producer and worker. It contains no business repositories or application services.
- `backend/notification-worker/`: RabbitMQ consumption and notification/email-job processing. It mutates only notification/inbox/job tables. It does not approve requests or adjust budgets.
- `frontend/`: Angular application. Authentication uses the framework-independent Keycloak JavaScript adapter, Authorization Code + PKCE, and in-memory tokens.
- `e2e/`: critical Playwright journeys against the actual local application. Infrastructure failure tests remain backend/system tests rather than fragile browser timing tests.
- Local infrastructure: PostgreSQL, Keycloak, RabbitMQ and Mailpit plus application/worker/frontend containers. Keycloak gets its own database credentials/database; application schema changes use Flyway.

The [decision log](DECISIONS.md) specifies tenant boundaries, lock order, outbox recovery, SMTP limitations and the researched version baseline. PostgreSQL remains the source of truth; the API can serve purchase operations while RabbitMQ or the notification worker is unavailable.

#### P2 endpoint/action and retry contract

Every route uses P1 validated membership/company scope. Own-request visibility follows identity within the current company, including after a primary-department change. Managers may read non-drafts in assigned departments. Finance detail/history visibility requires an amount above EUR 1,000, non-draft state and an actual recorded department approver: finance cannot inspect another user's draft, low-value request or a high-value request that has not reached the finance route. Administrator alone grants no request-content or approval authority. Foreign-company lookups return 404 before data is exposed; role/ownership failures return 403.

| Endpoint | Authority and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /api/requests` | Employee; title, justification and positive EUR amount only. Department comes from current membership. Required UUID `Idempotency-Key`; first success and replay return the original 201 response |
| `PUT /api/requests/{id}` | Employee and owner; draft only; expected version plus complete editable fields. Rebinds an old draft to the current primary department. No client company/department/usage/status fields |
| `POST /api/requests/{id}/submit` | Employee and owner; expected version, required UUID idempotency key; draft must still match current primary department, otherwise 409 until explicitly edited. Freezes submitted content/department |
| `POST /api/requests/{id}/cancel` | Owner; expected version; pending department/finance step only; no cancellation of a draft or terminal request |
| `POST /api/requests/{id}/reject` | Assigned current-step manager, or finance at the finance step; expected version and nonblank reason. Requester and prior department approver cannot reject the finance step |
| `GET /api/requests` | Paginated `view=mine` (default) or `view=inbox`, optional status filter. Inbox requires manager/finance authority, contains only actionable current-step requests, and excludes requester/prior-step actor |
| `GET /api/requests/{id}` and `/{id}/history` | Current company and the explicit own/manager/finance read scope above; another employee's own-company content is denied |

Pages are zero-based, sizes 1–100, ordered by creation time and ID for stable ties. States use explicit uppercase API enum values. Amounts fit NUMERIC(14,2), must be positive, and reject excess precision instead of rounding. Other writes use expected-version conflicts: retrying a completed edit/cancel/reject yields 409 without duplicate effects. A create/submit key is scoped to company and actor identity, binds the operation/target/canonical validated input, and cannot be reused for a different command. Concurrent identical keys wait on PostgreSQL uniqueness and replay the recorded response; failed transactions retain no key/result. Authorization is rechecked even on replay. No automatic key expiry is introduced in P2.

State/version, append-only history/audit, command result and any event/recipient rows commit together. Each successful write has a server-generated UUID correlation ID, available in the response/history/audit/event; replay keeps the original ID. Draft create/edit writes history/audit but no notification event. Approval endpoints, actual charges and broker publication remain P3/P4 work.

Fingerprints bind operation, target ID and canonical client fields (including the original expected version for submit), not mutable membership-derived department/role data. A matching successful replay bypasses new state/version/department eligibility checks but never current authentication, membership, role or ownership checks. The response is the original command result, not a promise of the resource's current state; clients may follow with GET. Database lock timeouts return 409 `retryable_conflict`; retry with the same key after the competing transaction settles. The loser cannot delete or overwrite the winner's result.

History contains version/action/from/to state, actor identity, timestamp, correlation and a rejection reason where applicable. It does not preserve old draft title/justification snapshots. History access is authorized from the current scoped request, not merely the history actor. Requests start at version 0; every successful edit/transition increments it exactly once.

#### P2 notification contract (schema version 1)

Envelope: stable event UUID, schema version, type, company/request IDs, correlation UUID, occurred-at Instant, minimal transition payload (from/to status, request version, actor identity), and explicit recipient identity/membership references with IN_APP and EMAIL channels. Do not copy justification, rejection reason, credentials or tokens into events.

- `request.submitted`: active assigned department managers other than the requester.
- `request.department_approved` (P3, only when finance remains): active finance approvers excluding requester and department approver, plus the active requester.
- `request.approved`, `request.rejected`, `request.cancelled`: use the locked transition's **previous** pending state to select valid step assignees, then union the active requester and active actual previous approvers; only then remove the acting identity and deduplicate. Active membership in the event company applies to every category; the requester need not retain EMPLOYEE. Excluding a prior approver from finance assignees must not exclude them as a historical participant.
- Recipients are captured under the authorization barrier at transition time. An event is still recorded if there are no eligible recipients. P4 must revalidate membership/company before materializing delivery and record skipped/ineligible recipients rather than redirecting to a different company. New assignments do not retroactively rewrite old events.

Outbox and recipient rows accumulate without RabbitMQ. Event identity/content and captured recipients are immutable; future relay delivery/lease metadata is separate from immutable event fields. History is ordered by request version, not an ambiguous timestamp tie.

- P2.1: draft/create/edit/submit/list/detail/cancel/reject commands with validation, pagination and explicit errors. Reject mutation of submitted amounts/departments, department spoofing, decimal overflow and excess precision; define retry semantics for create/submit before the UI depends on them.
- P2.2: allowed transition policy and immutable audit records carrying actor, company, action, timestamp and correlation identifier. Never log tokens or unnecessary request bodies.
- P2.3: define OpenAPI resources/error codes and event envelope: stable event ID, version, company, request ID, occurred-at timestamp and recipient references. Define notification recipients and expected channels for each transition.
- P2.4: establish state/audit/outbox persistence in one transaction from the first notification-producing command; publishing is introduced in P4. Add rollback and API retry/idempotency tests.

Exit: the implemented draft/submission/cancel/reject state/role matrix, amount validation, cross-company history reads and state/audit/outbox rollback tests pass. Approval threshold routes are specified here and exercised end to end in P3. API and event contracts are reviewed and stable enough for parallel UI/worker work. Outbox entries can accumulate safely without a broker.

- The worker owns relay, broker consumer and SMTP-job runners; the business app never opens broker/SMTP connections. A separate restricted worker database login can read canonical outbox/recipient and current membership metadata and mutate delivery tables, but not purchases, budgets, identities, roles, audit or immutable events. Migrations remain app-owned. Existing volumes require an explicit worker-role upgrade, never a silent reset.

Existing events are backfilled into dispatch; new capture initializes dispatch atomically without a broker dependency. Short database transactions claim due work with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, a unique fencing token, expiry and bounded attempts/backoff; completion/failure updates require the same live token. Expired claims are reclaimable. No database transaction spans broker/SMTP I/O.
- Publish the original stable schema-v1 event ID as a persistent JSON message to durable topology. Only an affirmative publisher confirm **and no mandatory return** permits marking dispatch published. Nack, return, timeout or crash leaves retry/recovery possible. A confirm-before-mark crash can publish twice; consumption must deduplicate, not pretend to prevent this window.
- The consumer validates schema/type/transition, transport identity and the exact envelope/recipient snapshot against the canonical company-scoped outbox record. Forged or unsupported messages cannot select arbitrary recipients or purchase data. Under the company authorization barrier it revalidates each captured membership/identity/company, then atomically commits one inbox marker and unique per-event/recipient/channel delivery outcomes, in-app records and SMTP jobs before ACK. Ineligible recipients receive durable SKIPPED outcomes, never rerouting to a new company; replay does not undo a skip or read state. Bounded consumer retries end in durable quarantine/dead-letter handling rather than hot requeue loops.
- SMTP jobs are separately leased and revalidate recipient eligibility at each claim. Network delivery occurs after that claim commits; revocation may race an already-authorized send. Local Mailpit recipients are deterministic fictional identity-UUID addresses under `demo.invalid`; no Keycloak email lookup or external real-recipient delivery is added. Stable Message-ID/correlation metadata supports observation but cannot guarantee exactly-once SMTP after acceptance-before-save failure.
